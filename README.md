## 📑 Core Concepts & Framework

### A. Severity vs. Seriousness
* **Severity:** Refers to the intensity or gradient of a specific event (e.g., *Mild, Moderate, Severe* head pain). A severe headache is not necessarily regulatory "serious."
* **Seriousness:** A regulatory classification based on patient outcomes that pose a threat to life or function.

### B. Regulatory Seriousness Criteria (ICH E2A / CIOMS)
An Adverse Event is classified as **SERIOUS** if it meets any of the following criteria:
1. **Death:** Results in patient mortality.
2. **Life-Threatening:** Places the patient at immediate risk of death at the time of the event.
3. **Inpatient Hospitalization / Prolongation:** Requires new hospital admission or extends an existing stay.
4. **Persistent or Significant Disability/Incapacity:** Substantial disruption of ability to conduct normal life functions.
5. **Congenital Anomaly / Birth Defect:** Structural/functional abnormality in offspring exposed in utero.
6. **Important Medical Event (IME):** May jeopardize patient safety or require medical/surgical intervention to prevent one of the outcomes listed above.

### C. Expectedness (Labeled vs. Unlabeled)
* **Expected (Labeled):** Event is listed in nature, severity, and frequency within the reference safety document (SmPC / IB / Package Insert).
* **Unexpected (Unlabeled):** Event is NOT listed or is more specific/severe than described in current reference safety information.
